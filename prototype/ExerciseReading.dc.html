Coral reefs cover less than one percent of the ocean floor, yet they support roughly a quarter of all known marine species. Their color comes from algae living inside the coral's tissue, in a relationship that benefits both — the algae get shelter, and the coral gets food. When ocean temperatures rise even slightly, that partnership can break down: the coral expels its algae, turns pale, and is left without its main energy source. This is what's known as bleaching, and while a bleached reef isn't necessarily dead, it is under serious stress.
